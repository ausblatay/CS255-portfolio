# CS255-portfolio

## Project Summary

The DriverPass project involved designing a system for a driving school that wanted to help students prepare for their driving tests and schedule driving lessons. The client needed a system that would allow customers to manage appointments, access online training materials and practice tests, and keep track of their progress. The system also needed to support employees and administrators by allowing them to manage schedules, customer information, training packages, and reports. My work included creating a business requirements document to identify what the client needed and a system design document to explain how those needs could be met.

## What I Did Well

I think one of the things I did well was breaking down the client's requirements and organizing them into a system that made sense. Instead of looking at DriverPass as just a website for scheduling lessons, I considered the different people who would use it and the tasks they would need to complete. Creating UML diagrams helped me understand how the different parts of the system would interact. I also learned how important it is to keep the design connected to the original requirements instead of adding features that the client did not ask for.

## What I Would Improve

If I could revise one part of my work, I would spend more time reviewing the UML diagrams and making sure every relationship and process was as clear as possible. The diagrams need to be understandable not only to the person creating them but also to the developers who may use them to build the actual system. I would review each diagram against the business requirements, check that important scenarios and exceptions are covered, and make sure the diagrams use consistent terminology. This would help reduce confusion during development and make future maintenance easier.

## Understanding and Implementing User Needs

I approached the DriverPass project by identifying the client's main goals and considering what customers, drivers, secretaries, owners, and IT personnel would need from the system. I then used those requirements to guide the system design, including appointment scheduling, training packages, online practice tests, progress tracking, and role-based access. Considering the user's needs is important because a system can be technically sound and still fail if it does not solve the problems people actually have. Understanding those needs early also helps prevent unnecessary features, wasted development time, and costly changes later.

## My Approach to Software Design

My approach to software design is to understand the problem first before deciding how to solve it. In future projects, I would start by gathering requirements, identifying the users and their responsibilities, and separating the system's functional requirements from its technical requirements. I would then use tools such as use case diagrams, activity diagrams, sequence diagrams, and class diagrams to map out how the system should work. I would also review the design against the original requirements and ask for feedback before moving forward. This project helped me see that software development is not just about writing code. Good planning, clear documentation, and communication are just as important to building a system that works well and can be maintained over time.
